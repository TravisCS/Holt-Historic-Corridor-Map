About the data

Map by T. C. Smith. Built November 1, 2026 for a proposal to the City of Ontario. Independent map. Not affiliated with or endorsed by the City of Ontario, the City of Pomona, Fairplex, SBCTA, Omnitrans, Metrolink, LA Metro or any business shown; it uses their public pages and data.

In short: this map is locked to one historic corridor, Holt Boulevard, the east-west road that the West Valley Connector bus rapid transit line runs on, from Ontario International Airport and the Ontario Convention Center in the east, through downtown Ontario on Euclid Avenue and Montclair, to downtown Pomona, the Pomona Transit Center, the Pomona Arts Colony and Fairplex in the west. It is for exploring and defining what a visitor partnership between the City of Ontario, the City of Pomona and Fairplex would contain: Pomona draws, Ontario hosts, transit connects.

Pomona drawsFairplex, the Knight Riders Cricket Ground, the LA County Fair, the Millard Sheets Art Center, the Arts Colony galleries and downtown Pomona dining.
Ontario hostsThe airport, the Convention Center and its expansion, the convention hotel, the hotel rooms along Holt, and the historic downtown on Euclid Avenue.
Transit connectsThe West Valley Connector on Holt, Metrolink and the Metro A Line at Pomona and Montclair, and the gap of 1.9 miles by street between the Pomona Transit Center and Fairplex.

How to read it

Hotel: color is the tier, size grows with rooms; the room count is on the card. A dashed outline means a hotel outside Ontario. Ring with a number: that many places grouped at this zoom (red hotels, green Pomona assets, teal businesses). Zoom in or tap it to split. Pomona asset (green) and Ontario asset (red): venues, districts and civic anchors with a card each. Business along Holt: small dot colored by category; dots gather into numbered bubbles when zoomed out. Holt spine: the corridor's centerline, Fairplex side of Pomona to Vineyard Avenue. West Valley Connector route and stations; Metrolink and Metro A Line stations are squares. The gap: the 2.0 miles by street (1.7 in a straight line) between the Pomona Transit Center and Fairplex Gate 1 that no frequent transit covers yet. The A Line gap (paler): the 2.4 miles in a straight line (2.7 by street) between the A Line's end at Pomona North and the Pomona Transit Center, linked today only by local Foothill Transit buses.

Notes

  • The view is locked to a box from the west side of Fairplex to Ontario International Airport, and from the Pomona North and Montclair stations in the north to the Ontario East Metrolink station in the south, so the map can be used to define the corridor without drifting off it.
  • Hotels are the properties from the Ontario Hotel Map that fall inside the box: Ontario hotels from the GOCAL roster and the City's info sheets, and Pomona, Montclair and Claremont hotels from directory listings checked against official sites and meetings directories, with room counts and sources on each card [1] [2] [3].
  • Pomona and Ontario assets are the venues, districts, stations and civic anchors a partnership would be built around; each card names the official page it was read from.
  • Downtown Ontario places are the restaurants, cafes, bars, markets, shops and civic or cultural spots inside the downtown box on Euclid Avenue and Holt. They come from a wider sweep of every named business within about a quarter mile of Holt (OpenStreetMap and public directories, November 1, 2026) that is kept in the data file; auto, services, health and education listings, and everything outside downtown Ontario, are left off the map because the proposal is about the two downtowns and the hotel district. Downtown Pomona's dining and galleries are on the Pomona assets layer.
  • High-speed rail and its links: Brightline West station, time and speed facts from Brightline West's FAQ and project overview; the ONT Connector from the state CEQA record SCH 2022070039; the Rancho Cucamonga to Pomona North service from SBCTA's project page. The rail lines on this layer are schematic, not engineered alignments, and no opening dates are stated beyond what those pages say.
  • The Holt spine is the road's centerline merged from OpenStreetMap way geometry [4]. The West Valley Connector alignment and stations follow SBCTA and Omnitrans [5] [6]; rail lines are drawn from OpenStreetMap track geometry and stations from Metrolink and LA Metro [7] [8].
  • City limits are the official boundaries from the Board of Equalization layer, loaded live with a saved copy as fallback [9].
  • When a hotel, business or dining card opens, its Google rating, review count, price band and current photo are fetched live from Google Places under the map owner's key and shown with a link to the Google listing; they are not stored in this repository [10].
  • Every link was checked by script and by AI research agents on November 2, 2026, and the ones that refuse scripts were opened in a browser; the full result, with every link and its verdict, is in the repository as link-audit-2026-11-02.md. Thirteen links were dead, moved or pointed at the wrong page and were replaced, updated or removed; a few publisher pages block automated readers and were checked for form only. Items that could not be verified say so on their cards: the cricket ground's seat count (Fairplex publishes none; the card gives the Associated Press's report of about 5,000 temporary seats) and its position (placed by this map at the center of the former racetrack infield), Craft Collective's current status, the convention hotel's 650 versus 600-plus rooms, the Arts District North boundary, and the Town Square farmers market.
  • On November 3, 2026 every source and every card link was checked again, and on November 7 the links for Ontario Mills, Victoria Gardens and Toyota Arena (added November 6): by script, in a web browser for the sites that refuse scripts, through the OpenStreetMap API for each OpenStreetMap object, and by reading each page for the name of the record that cites it. The result, with each source's check, is the source list (PDF).
  • On November 12, 2026 the corrections checked for the Holt Corridor Simple map on November 11 and 12 were carried into this map's cards where it shows the same record: moved links were repointed, sources were added where a card's wording rests on them, and wording, figures and two marker positions were corrected. The links new to this map were requested by script that day; the other links were not requested again.
  • Screenshot and right-click menu. The Screenshot link under the title takes a picture of the map exactly as it looks, with any open card and panel, and opens it in a new tab with a download button, the time, a link back to the same view, the layers shown, the card's text and the credits (html2canvas [17] through snapshot.js). Right-click the map (press and hold on a phone) to share the spot, copy a link that reopens the map there with a pin, copy its coordinates, open it in Google Maps, see what is there, or take a screenshot; it is the same menu as on the other maps by T. C. Smith.
  • Where each layer comes from. Every row in the Legend has a small + button. It opens a note on that layer: how far each source backs it, counted from the data, then each source with its date and link, what the layer is, why it matters and what to watch before relying on it. It never turns the layer on or off. Every card ends with a Sources list that names what each source gave to that card, links the pages read for it, and says what this map worked out itself. Both draw on the source list below.
